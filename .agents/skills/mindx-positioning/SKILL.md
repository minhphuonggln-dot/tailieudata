# MindX Positioning

## Objective

Make the lead magnet feel like a MindX asset, not a generic internet compilation.

## Required inputs

- Selected idea from `02_idea_shortlist.md`
- Persona/JTBD from `03_persona_jtbd.md`
- `brand_inputs/mindx_usp.md`
- `brand_inputs/content_direction.md`
- `brand_inputs/proof_assets.md`

## Workflow

1. **Read brand inputs first.** Use only approved USP, proof, tone, CTA, and claim boundaries.
2. **Identify the generic version.** Describe what typical internet content says about this topic.
3. **Define the MindX POV.** State what MindX believes, what it clarifies, and how it helps the learner make progress.
4. **Create a message house.** One-liner promise, main argument, 3 supporting points, and CTA bridge.
5. **Map proof.** Connect each claim to proof assets. If proof is missing, mark it as “requires approval” or rewrite safely.
6. **Define content boundaries.** List claims to avoid and terms that need careful explanation.
7. **Recommend CTA.** Make CTA contextual, useful, and not overly sales-heavy.

## Output format

Create/update `production_artifacts/04_positioning_brief.md` with:

- selected idea,
- MindX point of view,
- what generic content misses,
- MindX angle,
- message house,
- proof to include,
- claims to avoid,
- CTA.

## Rules

- Do not invent MindX facts, numbers, partnerships, student outcomes, mentor credentials, or hiring claims.
- Do not overpromise salary, job placement, or speed of career transition.
- Prefer practical, learner-centric positioning over hype.
- If `mindx_usp.md` is incomplete, create a “USP questions to fill” section and continue with safe placeholders.

## Quality checklist

- Positioning is meaningfully different from generic content.
- CTA follows from the reader's JTBD.
- Every MindX-specific claim has proof or approval status.
- Tone matches the brand inputs.
- The lead magnet can still deliver value even before the CTA.
